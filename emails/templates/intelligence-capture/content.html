Your drawings survive too. And they're searchable.

Sketch a mind map in a lecture, or a flowchart of what to do when a patient's blood sugar drops, and PlaceMate doesn't flatten it into text. It redraws it as a clean diagram, keeps it with your photographed page, and files it on the shift it came from.

The sketch on your page, and the same flowchart redrawn beside it

And because it's saved properly, you can ask for it back: weeks later, type “show me that sepsis mind map” and there it is, redrawn and readable, with a link to the shift you learned it on. Revision scribbles used to live and die in one notebook. Now they're a library.

Two honest things before you start snapping.

Before you snap, check nothing patient-identifiable is in frame. No names, NHS numbers or dates of birth, and no other paperwork behind your page. The app reminds you every single time, on purpose. This one is on all of us to get right.

It will still misread the odd word. Late-shift handwriting is a challenge for anyone. That's why nothing files without you, and why your photo stays alongside so you can always check against what you actually wrote.

While it's in beta 🌱  your photos are stored so you can check them against what we read, and the PlaceMate team may review them to make the reading better. Everything you file is still yours, exactly as before.
